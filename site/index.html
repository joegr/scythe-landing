scythe

Cut cards the way they should be cut.

Scythe is where your team cuts, marks up and files its evidence, writes its cases and prints both, on a laptop, a phone or a tablet. Every team gets its own private Scythe. Every card is the author’s exact words, every highlight lands where you put it, and nothing your partner cut ever gets lost.

What we believe

  1. A card is what the author wrote. Scythe only saves a card if its words are the article’s exact words. You can’t paraphrase inside a card or “clean up” a quote, and no AI ever rewrites one. Paste a passage in and Scythe finds it in the article, word for word.
  2. What you highlight is what you read. Your underlining and highlighting is your speech, so it has to be exact. Marks always cover whole words, they never slide around, and the read-aloud version is exactly what you marked.
  3. Your partner’s work never disappears. If you both edit the same card at once, Scythe shows you both versions and you decide: keep yours, keep theirs, or combine them. Every earlier version is kept.
  4. Every card belongs to a topic. Every past NSDA resolution for Policy, LD, PF and Big Questions is already built in, and you can add your own. Cards that aren’t filed under a topic are gone by February.
  5. If it’s in Scythe, you can print it. Each card and each case is saved as one plain text file, and the printout comes from that same file. What you print is exactly what you cut and marked, with nothing added, dropped or reformatted.

How it works

  • Find the article. Paste a link and Scythe opens the page and starts the cite for you: author, date and publication. Behind a paywall or in a PDF? Paste the text and Scythe fixes the broken line breaks. Only have it on paper? Take a photo of the page.
  • Cut. Select the part you want, write your tag, check the cite, and cut it.
  • Mark it up. Select words and press 1, 2 or 3. You’ll never highlight half a word by accident.
  • File it. Put it in a file, like Aff or Neg, then in the right block: inherency, solvency, a DA link. Mark it approved when it’s cut, checked and ready for rounds.
  • Answer it. Connect cards to each other: this one answers that one, this one backs it up. Every card shows its answers, so your frontlines are already there.
  • Find every card on an author. Tap a name in a card to see every card your team has that quotes that person or was cut from their writing.
  • Read it, print it, paste it. Read the full card or just the highlighted part. Print one card, a card with all its answers as frontlines, or a whole file as your speech doc. Or paste it into Word with Verbatim formatting.

Your evidence, kept yours

  • Your team gets its own Scythe. Your cards, their history and your members live there and nowhere else. Other teams can’t see what you’ve cut, not even the ones you’ll hit in elims.
  • Invite only. Nobody can make an account or see a single card without an invite code from one of your team’s admins. A code expires after two weeks, and an admin can cancel it sooner.
  • Coaches run the team. Admins, usually coaches, invite people, remove them and make other admins. Removing someone signs them out on every device at once. A team always keeps at least one admin, so it can never be locked out.
  • No password to steal. Sign in with a passkey, using Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode. If you’d rather use an email and password, Scythe stores the password scrambled so nobody can read it, Scythe included.
  • Every card can be checked. Each card is the author’s exact words with a cite to the article, and Scythe refuses to save one that isn’t. Every edit is signed with the username of whoever made it, and the card’s history shows all of them.
  • Students stay private. The history shows usernames, never real names or emails.

On your laptop and your phone

  • Anywhere you have a browser. Cut at your desk, check a card in the hallway before round. It’s the same cards and the same history everywhere. On an iPhone, add Scythe to your Home Screen and it opens like an app. A native iPhone app is in testing.
  • Scan a printed card. Got a card on paper from camp or an old tub? On your phone or tablet, tap “Scan a card” and take a picture, or pick one you’ve already saved. Your team’s own Scythe reads the words, and no outside service ever sees the photo. You check what it read, then save it as a card.
  • Nudges, if you want them. Turn them on from your account and Scythe reminds you after three days without cutting a card, then once a week, four times at most. It comes by email, or as a notification on your laptop or phone. Coaches get one when none of their debaters has cut a card in three days. Nudges are off unless you turn them on. Turning them on shares only your username, your team’s name, your role and your email, and nothing about your cards. A small AI writes the one-line reminder. All it knows is your username, your team’s name, whether you’re a debater or a coach, and how many days it’s been.

A sample nudge: “ada, it’s been 10 days since your last card. The infonets are hungry: store one today?”

Scythe learns how your team cuts

Once your team has approved 50 cards, Scythe can learn from them and suggest underlining and highlighting on new cards, shown as dotted underlines. Press Enter to keep a suggestion or x to drop it, and a dropped one never comes back on that card. Nothing changes on a card until you say yes. Scythe only learns from cards your team approved, and it never changes a word.

Scythe also picks out the people, organizations, places and dates in each card, so writing cites goes faster.

Write your case in Scythe

A simple editor for writing your case, with your team’s cards right beside it. Find a card, select the words you’ll read, and they go into your case as the card’s exact words, linked back to that card, so you and your partner can always see where a line came from. Scythe won’t let a misquote into a case. Cases print the same way cards do, straight from the one file they’re saved in.

Who it’s for

Policy, LD and PF debaters who cut their own cards. Coaches who want to run who’s on the team, see what everyone has cut, and know which cards are ready for rounds. Partners finishing the same file at 1 a.m. before a tournament.
