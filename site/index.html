Evidence is verbatim, or it isn’t evidence.

Scythe is a card cutter for high school and college debaters. Cut the author’s exact words, mark them up on whole words, file them into your blocks, and walk into round knowing every card says what the article says.

What we believe

  1. Never rewrite the author. Scythe cuts the exact characters of the source. You can’t paraphrase inside a card, and a pasted quote is matched back to the article, curly quotes, dashes and all, so you can’t “clean it up” either.
  2. Your highlighting is your argument. What you underline and highlight is what you read in the speech, so Scythe treats it as real data. Every mark snaps to whole words, nothing drifts, and the read-aloud version is exactly what you marked.
  3. Nobody loses a card because their partner saved first. If two of you edit the same card in the same second, Scythe stops the second save and shows both changes. Add yours to theirs, keep theirs, or keep yours. The other version stays in the card’s history either way.
  4. Your team owns its evidence. Cards live in a GitHub repository your team controls, not in a company’s database. If Scythe disappeared tomorrow, every card, cite and highlight would still be there as plain files.
  5. Every card has a resolution. All 542 past NSDA topics (Policy, LD, PF and Big Questions) are built in, and you can write your own. A card that isn’t filed under a topic gets lost by February.

How it works

  • Open the article. Paste a link and Scythe reads the page for you, or paste the text from a paywalled article or a PDF. The source gets a cite drafted from its author, date and publication.
  • Cut. Select the passage, or paste one you copied earlier and Scythe finds it. Write the tag, check the cite, cut. The card is the source’s words, nothing else.
  • Mark it up. Select and press 1, 2 or 3. Selections grow to whole words, so you never highlight half of “billions”.
  • File it. Aff or Neg, then the block: Inherency, Solvency, the DA links. Approve it when it’s ready for rounds.
  • Answer it. Link cards to each other: this card answers that one, supports it, contradicts it. Each card lists its answers, so frontlines write themselves.
  • Read it, print it, paste it. Full card, read-aloud, or plain text. Copy into Word with Verbatim formatting (cyan highlight, boxed emphasis, small unread text) or print a whole file as a speech doc.
  • See who changed what. Every change is saved with your name and the time, so a card’s whole history is a click away.

Scythe reads with you

Under the cutter is spaCy, the language library researchers use. It checks the words in every card you save, so your marks land on Scythe’s word boundaries. It also finds the people, organizations, places and dates in a card: the start of your quals.

Coming next: suggested highlights that learn from your team’s approved cards, and finding every card that quotes the same person. They’re written down as tests before a line of them is built.

What Scythe will never do

  • Rewrite, summarize or “improve” a card. There is no AI writing anywhere in Scythe.
  • Keep your evidence somewhere you can’t take it with you.
  • Throw away a save without telling you whose save won.

Who it’s for

Policy, LD and PF debaters who cut their own evidence. Coaches who want to see what their squad cut and approve what’s ready. Partners prepping the same file at 1 a.m. And anyone labeling text who wants their labels to mean something: every card you cut can also become training data.
