scythe

Cut cards the way they should be cut.

Scythe is where your team cuts, marks up and files evidence, on your laptop or your iPhone. Every team gets its own private Scythe. Every card is the author’s exact words, every highlight lands where you put it, and nothing your partner cut ever gets lost.

What we believe

  1. A card is what the author wrote. Scythe only lets you cut the author’s own words. You can’t paraphrase inside a card or “clean up” a quote, and no AI ever rewrites one. If you paste something in, Scythe checks it against the article, word for word.
  2. What you highlight is what you read. Your underlining and highlighting is your speech, so it should be exact. Marks always cover whole words, they never slide around, and the read-aloud version is exactly what you marked.
  3. Your partner’s work never disappears. If you both edit the same card at once, Scythe shows you both versions and you decide: keep yours, keep theirs, or combine them. Older versions are always saved.
  4. Every card belongs to a topic. Every past NSDA resolution for Policy, LD, PF and Big Questions is already built in, and you can add your own. Cards that aren’t filed under a topic are gone by February.
  5. If it’s in Scythe, you can print it. Each card is saved as one plain text file, and the printout comes from that same file. What you print is always exactly what you cut and marked, with nothing added, dropped or reformatted.

How it works

  • Find the article. Paste a link, or paste the text if it’s behind a paywall or in a PDF. Scythe starts the cite for you with the author, date and publication.
  • Cut. Select the part you want, write your tag, check the cite, and cut it.
  • Mark it up. Select words and press 1, 2 or 3. You’ll never highlight half a word by accident.
  • File it. Put it on the aff or the neg, then in the right block: inherency, solvency, a DA link. Mark it ready when it’s good to read in round.
  • Answer it. Connect cards to each other: this one answers that one, this one backs it up. Every card shows its answers, so your frontlines are already there.
  • Read it, print it, paste it. Read the full card or just the highlighted part. Print one card, a card with all its answers, or a whole file as your speech doc. Or paste it into Word with Verbatim formatting.

Your evidence, kept yours

  • Your team gets its own Scythe. Your cards, their history and your members live there and nowhere else. Other teams can’t see what you’ve cut, not even the ones you’ll hit in elims.
  • Invite only. Nobody can make an account without an invite code from one of your team’s admins. A code expires after two weeks, and an admin can cancel it sooner.
  • Coaches run the team. Admins, usually coaches, invite people, remove them and make other admins. Removing someone signs them out on every device at once. A team always keeps at least one admin, so it can never be locked out.
  • No password to steal. Sign in with a passkey and your phone keeps the key. If you use email and a password instead, Scythe stores the password scrambled so nobody can read it, Scythe included.
  • Every card can be checked. Each card is the author’s exact words with a cite to the article. Every edit is signed with the username of whoever made it, and the card’s history shows all of them. A card marked approved has been cut and checked, and is ready for rounds.
  • Students stay private. The history shows usernames, never real names or emails.

On your laptop and your phone

  • Web and iPhone. Cut at your desk, check a card in the hallway before round. It’s the same cards and the same history on both.
  • Scan a printed card. Got a card on paper from camp or an old tub? On your phone or tablet, tap “Scan a card” and take a picture. Your team’s own Scythe reads the words, with no outside service seeing the photo. You check what it read, then save it as a card.
  • Sign in with Face ID. Use a passkey and you just look at your phone, with no password to forget or share. Email and password works too, and so does Sign in with Apple once you’ve set up a passkey.
  • Nudges, if you want them. Turn them on from your account and Scythe emails you after three days without cutting a card, then once a week, four times at most. Coaches get one when none of their debaters has cut a card in three days. Nudges are off unless you turn them on. Turning them on shares only your username, your team’s name, your role and your email, and nothing about your cards. A small AI writes the one-line reminder. All it knows is your username, your team’s name, whether you’re a debater or a coach, and how many days it’s been. It never sees your cards.
